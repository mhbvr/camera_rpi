Focus value

A sharpness score computed by the Pi's image processor from the picture itself (libcamera FocusFoM). It does not come from the lens: the HQ camera has no autofocus.

  • Higher = sharper. Turn the focus ring until the value peaks.
  • Centre only: measured in the middle third of the height and the middle half of the width. Put the subject there.
  • No unit or absolute scale: the value depends on scene detail, brightness and exposure. Compare values only for the same scene and settings.
  • Noise raises it: with high gain the grain counts as "detail". Focus in good light, and press Lock current first so exposure stays fixed while you turn the ring.

White balance
Image

Processing applied by the image processor to the stream and to snapshots. It does not change exposure.

How exposure works

Auto: each frame, the auto-exposure algorithm measures brightness, multiplies its brightness target by 2EV, and computes the total exposure (shutter × gain) needed. It raises the shutter first, then the gain, step by step along the selected exposure profile. Two limits cap it: the frame time (1/fps) caps the shutter, and the profile's last step caps both. Past the analogue limit, digital gain makes up the rest.

Manual: shutter and gain are used as set; EV, metering and profile have no effect. A shutter longer than the frame time lowers the frame rate.

When auto exposure reaches its limits, a warning appears under Actual values.

Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.6

Frame rate

The frame time (1/fps) is also the longest possible shutter: at 30 fps the shutter cannot exceed about 33 ms, in auto or manual mode.

  • Auto, dark scene: a lower frame rate lets auto exposure use a longer shutter (up to the profile maximum) before raising the gain.
  • Manual: a shutter longer than the frame time lowers the frame rate to fit.
  • Changing the frame rate restarts the encoder (brief interruption).

Shutter (manual)

Exposure time per frame, shown as 1/x s. It is used only when auto exposure is off. A shutter longer than the frame time (1/fps) lowers the frame rate to fit; the encoder restarts. Longer shutter = brighter, but more motion blur.

Gain (manual)

Sensor amplification (analogue gain; 1 = base sensitivity). It is used only when auto exposure is off. Higher gain = brighter, but noisier. The sensor maximum is ≈22.

EV compensation (auto only)

Multiplies the brightness target of auto exposure by 2EV (+1 = twice as bright, −1 = half). It is disabled in manual mode.

  • The target is capped at 90 % of full scale, so values above about +2.4 have no further effect.
  • When auto exposure is at its limits (dark scene: shutter at the frame time, gain at the profile maximum), EV has no effect: the target is already out of reach. A warning under Actual values shows this.

Metering (auto only)

Decides which parts of the image auto exposure looks at when it judges brightness. The Pi 4 splits the frame into 15 zones. Auto exposure averages their brightness, each pixel counted with its zone's weight, and steers that average to its target. Weight 0 means the zone is ignored.

Zone weights not available (no tuning data from the camera).
  • centre (centre-weighted): the middle counts most, edges less, corners not at all. Good default.
  • spot: only a small area in the middle. Use it when the subject is small and centred against a much brighter or darker background.
  • matrix: the whole frame evenly (plain average).

A bright window or dark corner in a zone with weight 0 does not change the exposure. Independently of metering, a histogram rule raises exposure if even the brightest 2 % of pixels are dim. In manual mode metering has no effect.

Exposure profiles (auto only)

A profile is a list of steps. To brighten the image, auto exposure raises the shutter to the next step, then the gain to the next step, and so on. The last step is the maximum. The shutter is also capped by the frame time.

Profile data not available (no tuning data from the camera).

Read at startup from the camera's tuning file . libcamera's camera API does not expose these profiles.

Actual values

What the camera really used for recent frames (libcamera metadata, about twice a second). Compare them with your settings: a difference means a value was clamped.

  • Frame time: actual frame duration; it is the shutter's upper limit.
  • Digital gain: extra gain applied by the image processor; 1.0 normally.
  • AE state: Idle (manual), Searching or Converged. Converged only means the values stopped changing; it is also reported when auto exposure is stuck at its limits.

At the limit means shutter ≈ min(frame time, profile maximum) and gain ≈ profile maximum. Then the image cannot get brighter in auto mode: lower the frame rate, use the "long" profile with a low enough frame rate, or switch to manual.

Lux

An estimate of scene brightness worked out by libcamera from the picture. The camera has no light sensor. It compares the current frame with a reference frame recorded during calibration:

lux = 830 × (27.2 ms / shutter) × (1 / gain) × (brightness / reference brightness)

  • Relative, not absolute: the formula assumes the lens and aperture used for calibration. On your lens, closing the aperture one stop halves the value; an ND filter lowers it too.
  • Reflected light: it uses the average brightness of the whole frame, so a white wall reads higher than dark cloth under the same light.
  • Less reliable with clipped highlights or a very dark, noisy image.
  • libcamera also uses it: auto exposure picks its brightness target, and auto white balance its colour priors, by lux.

Image processing

These settings change how the image processor renders the picture:

  • Brightness and contrast reshape the tone curve (gamma).
  • Saturation scales colour in the colour-correction matrix.
  • Sharpness and denoise set the strength of the sharpening and noise-reduction blocks.

They apply live, to both the stream and snapshots, and work in auto and manual exposure. They do not change exposure: no extra light reaches the sensor and no hidden detail is recovered. For a brighter exposure use EV, shutter or gain.

Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Brightness

Shifts the output levels up or down after the tone curve: every value moves by brightness × full scale (−1 … +1, default 0).

  • +0.2 lifts blacks to grey (washed out); −0.2 crushes shadows to black. Values are clipped at black and white.
  • This is not exposure: the sensor gets no extra light and dark detail is not recovered. To brighten a dark scene use EV, shutter or gain.

how it is applied · docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Contrast

Scales the tone curve around mid-grey: (value − 50 %) × contrast + 50 % (default 1).

  • > 1: darker darks and brighter brights, with more clipping at both ends.
  • < 1: flatter image; 0 is uniform grey.
  • Independently, an automatic contrast enhancement is always on (HQ tuning): it pulls the darkest 1 % of pixels down and the brightest 5 % up, by a limited amount, keeping the median where it is.

manual contrast · automatic enhancement · docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Saturation

Scales colour intensity: the colour-correction matrix is converted to luma/chroma, the chroma is multiplied by the saturation, and the result is converted back (default 1).

  • 0 = black and white; 1 = as tuned; 2 = twice the colour intensity.
  • Brightness (luma) is unchanged. Very high values posterise strong colours.

how it is applied · docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Sharpness

Strength of the image processor's sharpening filter (default 1, 0 = off). For a setting s, the sharpening gain is multiplied by s, its limit by √s, and its threshold divided by √s, so higher values also sharpen finer, lower-contrast detail.

  • Base values from the HQ tuning: threshold 0.75, strength 1.0, limit 0.5.
  • In this app's 2×2-binned sensor mode, libcamera halves strength and limit and doubles the threshold.
  • Too much sharpening exaggerates noise and edge halos, and costs bitrate. For focusing, the focus value is not affected by this setting.

Denoise

The Pi 4 image processor has two noise filters:

  • Spatial denoise on the raw sensor data. Its strength follows the sensor's noise model, so it automatically gets stronger at higher gain.
  • Colour denoise removes coloured blotches, in a fast or a high quality version.
