Focus value

A sharpness score computed by the Pi's image processor from the picture itself (libcamera FocusFoM). It does not come from the lens: the HQ camera has no autofocus.

  • Higher = sharper. Turn the focus ring until the value peaks.
  • Centre only: measured in the middle third of the height and the middle half of the width. Put the subject there.
  • No unit or absolute scale: the value depends on scene detail, brightness and exposure. Compare values only for the same scene and settings.
  • Noise raises it: with high gain the grain counts as "detail". Focus in good light, and press Lock current first so exposure stays fixed while you turn the ring.

White balance

Image

Processing applied by the image processor to the stream and to snapshots. It does not change exposure.

How exposure works

Auto: each frame, the auto-exposure algorithm (rpi.agc) measures brightness, multiplies its brightness target by 2EV, and computes the total exposure (shutter × gain) needed. It raises the shutter first, then the gain, step by step along the selected exposure profile. Two limits cap it: the frame time (1/fps) caps the shutter, and the profile's last step caps shutter and gain. Past the sensor's analogue limit, digital gain makes up the rest.

Manual: shutter and gain are used as set; EV, metering and profile have no effect. A shutter longer than the frame time lowers the frame rate.

Parameter names in these popups refer to the camera's libcamera tuning file. For the HQ camera on a Pi 4 that is usually /usr/share/libcamera/ipa/rpi/vc4/imx477.json; libcamera logs the path as "Using tuning file" (see the camweb log). Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.6

Frame rate

The frame time (1/fps) is also the longest possible shutter, in auto and manual mode: at 30 fps the shutter cannot exceed about 33 ms.

  • Auto, dark scene: a lower frame rate lets auto exposure use a longer shutter (up to the profile's last shutter step) before raising the gain.
  • Manual: a shutter longer than the frame time lowers the frame rate to fit.
  • Changing the frame rate restarts the encoder (brief interruption).

Shutter (manual)

Exposure time per frame, shown as 1/x s. It is used only when auto exposure is off. A shutter longer than the frame time (1/fps) lowers the frame rate to fit, and the encoder restarts. A longer shutter gives a brighter image but more motion blur.

Gain (manual)

Sensor amplification (analogue gain; 1 = base sensitivity). It is used only when auto exposure is off. Higher gain gives a brighter but noisier image. The sensor maximum, from libcamera's AnalogueGain control range, is reported by the camera.

EV compensation (auto only)

Multiplies the brightness target of auto exposure by 2EV (+1 = twice as bright, −1 = half). It is disabled in manual mode.

  • The base target is rpi.agc → y_target (by lux), scaled by rpi.agc → base_ev. libcamera caps the result at 90 % of full scale, so EV above about log2(0.9 / y_target) has no further effect.
  • When auto exposure is at its limits (dark scene: shutter at the frame time, gain at the profile's last step), EV has no effect: the target is already out of reach.

Metering (auto only)

Decides which parts of the image auto exposure looks at when it judges brightness. The Pi 4 splits the frame into 15 zones (numbered below). Auto exposure averages the brightness, each pixel counted with its zone's weight, and steers that average to its target. Weight 0 means the zone is ignored.

Each mode is a list of 15 weights in zone order 0–14: rpi.agc → metering_modes → centre-weighted / spot / matrix → weights. As the names say: centre favours the middle, spot a small central area, matrix the whole frame.

Independently of metering, histogram rules (rpi.agc → constraint_modes; the first mode in the file is used) can raise or lower exposure, for example to keep some pixels bright. In manual mode metering has no effect.

Exposure profiles (auto only)

A profile is a list of steps: shutter times (µs) and gains, in rpi.agc → exposure_modes → normal / short / long → shutter, gain. To brighten the image, auto exposure raises the shutter to the next step, then the gain to the next step, and so on. The last step is the maximum. The shutter is also capped by the frame time.

  • short reaches higher gain sooner (less motion blur, more noise).
  • long usually allows the longest shutter, which only helps if the frame rate is low enough.

Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.6

Actual values

What the camera really used for recent frames (libcamera metadata, about twice a second). Compare them with your settings: a difference means a value was clamped.

  • Frame time: actual frame duration; it is the shutter's upper limit.
  • Digital gain: extra gain applied by the image processor; 1.0 normally.
  • AE state: Idle (manual), Searching or Converged. Converged only means the values stopped changing; it is also reported when auto exposure is stuck at its limits.

A hint appears when the shutter has reached the frame time: from then on only gain can brighten the image, up to the last gain step of the profile (rpi.agc → exposure_modes). The profile's own shutter limit can be lower than the frame time at low frame rates; the UI cannot see that.

Lux

An estimate of scene brightness worked out by libcamera (rpi.lux) from the picture. The camera has no light sensor. It compares the current frame with a reference frame from calibration:

lux = reference_lux × (reference_shutter_speed / shutter) × (reference_gain / gain) × (Y / reference_Y)

The reference values are rpi.lux → reference_* in the tuning file. Y is the average brightness of the whole frame.

  • Relative, not absolute: the formula assumes the lens and aperture used for calibration. On your lens, closing the aperture one stop halves the value.
  • Reflected light: a white wall reads higher than dark cloth under the same light.
  • libcamera also uses it: auto exposure's target (rpi.agc → y_target) and auto white balance's priors (rpi.awb → priors) depend on lux.

Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.7

Image processing

These settings change how the image processor renders the picture:

  • Brightness and contrast reshape the tone curve (rpi.contrast).
  • Saturation scales colour in the colour-correction matrix (rpi.ccm).
  • Sharpness and denoise set the strength of the sharpening (rpi.sharpen) and noise-reduction (rpi.sdn) blocks.

They apply live, to the stream and snapshots, in auto and manual exposure. They do not change exposure: no extra light reaches the sensor and no hidden detail is recovered. For a brighter exposure use EV, shutter or gain.

Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Brightness

Shifts the output levels up or down after the tone curve: every value moves by brightness × full scale (−1 … +1, default 0).

  • Positive values lift blacks towards grey (washed out); negative values crush shadows. Values are clipped at black and white.
  • This is not exposure: no dark detail is recovered. To brighten a dark scene use EV, shutter or gain.

Applied by rpi.contrast on top of rpi.contrast → gamma_curve. Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Contrast

Scales the tone curve around mid-grey: (value − 50 %) × contrast + 50 % (default 1).

  • > 1: darker darks and brighter brights, with more clipping at both ends.
  • < 1: flatter image; 0 is uniform grey.
  • Separately, libcamera can apply an automatic contrast enhancement that stretches the darkest and brightest parts of the histogram while keeping the median. It is switched by rpi.contrast → ce_enable and tuned by lo_histogram, lo_level, lo_max, hi_histogram, hi_level, hi_max. The UI cannot change it.

Base curve: rpi.contrast → gamma_curve. Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Saturation

Scales colour intensity (chroma) inside the colour-correction matrix; brightness is unchanged (default 1).

  • 0 = black and white; 1 = as calibrated; 2 = twice the colour intensity.
  • The matrix itself is chosen by colour temperature from rpi.ccm → ccms. An optional rpi.ccm → saturation curve can also vary saturation with lux.

Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Sharpness

Strength of the image processor's sharpening filter (default 1, 0 = off). For a setting s, the filter's strength is multiplied by s, its limit by √s, and its threshold divided by √s, so higher values also sharpen finer, lower-contrast detail.

  • The base values are rpi.sharpen → threshold, strength, limit.
  • In binned sensor modes (this app uses a 2×2-binned mode) libcamera tones the sharpening down.
  • Too much sharpening exaggerates noise and edge halos, and costs bitrate. It does not affect the focus value.

Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.9

Denoise

The Pi 4 image processor has two noise filters:

  • Spatial denoise on the raw sensor data. Its strength follows the sensor noise model (rpi.noise), so it gets stronger automatically at higher gain. It is tuned by rpi.sdn → deviation, strength.
  • Colour denoise removes coloured blotches, in a fast or a high quality version.

White balance

Makes neutral (grey/white) objects look neutral under different light. It multiplies the red and blue channels by colour gains (green stays at 1). The colour-correction matrix (rpi.ccm) is then chosen for the resulting colour temperature.

Auto (rpi.awb, Bayesian search):

  • The image is split into 16 × 12 zones; zones that are too dark or have too few pixels are ignored (min_G, min_pixels).
  • For colour temperatures in the selected mode's range, it checks how grey the zones would become with the gains calibrated for that temperature (ct_curve). It weighs that against how likely each light is at the current lux level (priors).
  • It refines slightly off the calibrated curve for green/magenta tints (transverse_pos, transverse_neg), and moves to the result smoothly (speed, frame_period).

Manual: the red and blue gains are used as set; the colour temperature is estimated back from them.

Colour temp. in Actual values is the temperature libcamera is using now. A large uniform coloured area (a red wall) can fool auto white balance; then use a fixed mode, or Lock current while a neutral scene is in view.

Parameters: rpi.awb → … in the tuning file. Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.10

White balance modes (auto only)

A mode limits the range of colour temperatures auto white balance may choose: rpi.awb → modes → <mode> → lo, hi (kelvin). A narrow range (e.g. daylight) keeps colours stable when the scene content would otherwise mislead it. auto is the widest range.

Details: docs/DESIGN.md §3.10

Manual colour gains

Used when auto white balance is off. Each value multiplies its channel relative to green (1.0). Raise red if the image is too blue/cyan, and raise blue if it is too yellow/orange. Warm light (bulbs) needs a lower red and a higher blue gain than daylight.

The calibrated gains for a light of temperature T are sensitivity_r / r(T) and sensitivity_b / b(T), from rpi.awb → ct_curve (rows of T, r, b).

The easiest way to set them: point at a neutral scene in auto mode, then press Lock current.
